Agency probes — beneath the STAR answer
Your behavioral interview already extracts "X did Y to reach Z." These probes go underneath that answer. After the candidate gives their STAR story, pick one or two probes to surface agency signal the STAR doesn't reach.
Initiative
STAR captures: what they shipped. Misses whether anyone asked them to.
→  Who asked you to start this?
→  What would have happened if you hadn't?
→  What did you give up to make space for it?
Listen for: Nobody asked. Real cost named — other work paused, political capital spent. Weak: "it was part of my role," or no opportunity cost named.
Verification
STAR captures: what shipped. Misses what almost broke.
→  What did you check before you knew it was ready?
→  What did you almost miss?
→  How did you find the things you found?
Listen for: Specific verification practices (tests, peer checks, edge cases), at least one near-miss with the catch mechanism named. Weak: "I reviewed it carefully," or no near-misses ever happen.
Taste
STAR captures: that Z worked. Misses whether Z was the right call.
→  What were the alternatives to Z?
→  Knowing what you know now, would you do it the same way?
→  What's a similar piece of work you've seen that you think is better than yours?
Listen for: Alternatives considered and rejected for stated reasons. Willingness to name what they'd change. Named external work with reasoning. Weak: "this was the right approach" with no alternatives, or no retrospective updates.
Question quality
STAR captures: the questions they answered. Misses the questions they asked.
→  What was the question that reframed this work for you?
→  Whose question were you trying to answer? Was it the right one?
→  What's a question you wish someone had asked you at the start?
Listen for: Meta-questions about the work itself. Willingness to challenge the framing they were given. Evolved understanding. Weak: never questioned the goal, no reframing moments.
Feedback metabolism
STAR captures: the success story. Demands a symmetric failure story.
→  Name a decision you got wrong on a similar project. What did you do next?
→  What feedback during this work made you change course?
→  What did your team tell you afterward that you wished they'd said earlier?
Listen for: Specific wrong decision named cleanly. Specific behavior change. Openness to peer feedback. Weak: "it was actually the right call in hindsight," no specific updates, "my team didn't really have feedback."
Ambiguity tolerance
STAR captures: the executed path. Misses what was unknown when they started.
→  What didn't you know when you started?
→  What did you decide without authority?
→  What were you afraid would go wrong?
Listen for: Real unknowns named at the start. Decisions made under uncertainty. Specific fears that turned out real or unfounded. Weak: "I had all the information I needed," no decisions made without permission.
Ownership
STAR captures: what they did. Misses what they're responsible for.
→  Who else was involved? What's the part that was yours?
→  When this thing has problems, who picks it up?
→  If this had failed, what would you have told the team?
Listen for: Clean attribution. Named ongoing responsibility. Willingness to own a hypothetical failure. Weak: "we did this together" without specificity, no ongoing accountability, deflection on the hypothetical.
Scope expansion
STAR captures: work inside the lane. Misses what they took on that wasn't theirs.
→  Was this in your formal scope when you started?
→  What else were you doing that wasn't in your job description?
→  Did anyone push back? How did you handle that?
Listen for: Specific scope expansion with a name and a date. Things they did that nobody asked for. Real political negotiation. Weak: "my manager was supportive of growth," no out-of-scope work, no friction.
